Our Leadership

Holden

First Minister & Founder

Holden founded the Blahaj Church in 2024 in the San Francisco Bay Area. While on mental health leave, Holden sought to reconcile deeply held spiritual beliefs about the sacredness of trans people with the hostility of the modern world. What began as a personal search for meaning became a calling to build a spiritual home for others who shared those beliefs.

As First Minister, Holden leads the congregation's worship services, oversees the church's spiritual direction, and works with the board to advance the church's mission of affirming the sacred nature of gender diversity.

Board of Directors

The Blahaj Church is governed by a five-member board, the majority of whom are transgender, gender non-conforming, gender variant, or intersex individuals. Board members serve one-year terms and are responsible for overseeing the church's operations, setting policy, and ensuring the church remains true to its mission.

Our Story

The Blahaj Church was founded in 2024 in the San Francisco Bay Area by Holden, our First Minister. The church was born out of a deeply personal spiritual journey – a search for a faith community that did not merely tolerate trans people but recognized their existence as sacred.

While on mental health leave, Holden found that existing religious institutions either rejected trans people outright or offered only grudging acceptance. Neither reflected what Holden knew to be true: that gender diversity is woven into the fabric of human existence, that it appears across every culture and era, and that the divine is reflected in the full spectrum of gender identity. From that conviction, the Blahaj Church was born.

The church is incorporated in Sacramento County, California, and holds monthly worship gatherings in the Bay Area, alternating between Oakland (East Bay) and the greater San Francisco area (West Bay) so that congregants from across the region can participate. The congregation also gathers weekly online for virtual worship.

Though the congregation is still young and small, it carries forward one of the oldest spiritual traditions on earth: the honoring of those who transcend the boundaries of gender. Our name comes from the Blahaj – the beloved IKEA stuffed shark that became a symbol of comfort and solidarity for trans people around the world during the isolation of the COVID-19 pandemic.

We are building something real, one gathering at a time.

Affiliation

The Blahaj Church maintains an affiliation with the Sparkling Pink Pandas (SPP), a trans-focused motorcycle and scooter group based in San Francisco. Several congregants are also members of SPP, and the church participates in San Francisco Pride and Trans March through the club each June. SPP is not a ministry of the church – it is an independent community organization with its own history and mission – but the two groups share members, values, and a commitment to the safety and visibility of trans people.
